A love letter to the family you haven't started yet

To bring life into the world is an act of courage and hope.

Not because the world is finished, but because it isn't — and every child is a hand that helps build what comes next.

Begin reading

A gentler answer to a loud fear

Every generation has been told its moment is the wrong one. Ours hears it too — in headlines, in group chats, in the quiet math of what a good life is supposed to cost. It's a reasonable fear. It's also not the whole story.

On uncertainty

No generation has ever raised a child with full certainty about tomorrow. Ours won't be the first to manage it. Uncertainty has never been a reason to stop loving — it's usually the reason love matters.

On being ready

There is no finish line called ready. The people who raised you weren't finished becoming themselves either. A family isn't the reward for a completed life — it's often where the real completing happens.

On what lasts

Careers end. Trends pass. But a child who grows up knowing they were wanted carries that knowledge into every room they'll ever enter, long after we're gone. Few things we build outlast that.

The ordinary days are the ones you'll remember

A small hand learning to crack an egg, and the mess that means it worked.

Bare feet on warm grass, a whole afternoon with nowhere else to be.

One more chapter, even though you both know how it ends.

A living room full of noise you used to crave quiet from — and now can't imagine without.
